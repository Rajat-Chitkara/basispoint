Engagement

How an engagement works

Five stages. The scoping call is free and carries no obligation — if the question cannot be answered well from available primary sources, you will be told that at stage one rather than at delivery.

1

Scope

A call to establish the decision the research has to support. Output is a written scope, a fixed fee and a delivery date.

2

Sources

The source set is identified and confirmed with you before analysis starts, so you know what the answer will rest on.

3

Build

Filings read, figures extracted and cross-verified, model built. One mid-point check-in on direction.

4

Review

Draft delivered for questions. One round of revision on scope-consistent changes is included.

5

Deliver

Final report, working model, sources appendix and a walkthrough call to defend the numbers directly.

What you receive

  • The report — written to be read by a decision-maker, not padded to a page count.
  • The working model — a live spreadsheet with a single labelled assumptions block. Nothing hard-coded into formulas; you can change an input and watch the answer move.
  • A sources appendix — every document used, with enough detail to retrieve each one yourself.
  • A walkthrough call — the numbers defended live, by the person who read the filings.

Terms

  • Fees are fixed and quoted per engagement against the written scope — not billed by the hour. The day-ranges above indicate typical duration, not price.
  • Work is confidential. Commissioned research is yours; it is not republished, resold, or reused in a published issue.
  • Conclusions are not for sale. The scope is agreed in advance; the finding is whatever the sources support. If the answer is unwelcome, you still get the answer.
  • Capacity is limited. A small number of engagements run at a time, so start dates are real dates.

Is this a fit?

It works well when the data exists in filings, disclosures or audited accounts but is scattered, unaggregated, or contradicted by the commonly cited figure — and when the answer has to survive being questioned by an investment committee or a board.

It works poorly when the question needs primary interviews, consumer surveys, or proprietary transaction data that no one is required to file. Where that is the case you will be told at the scoping call, not sold an engagement that cannot deliver.
